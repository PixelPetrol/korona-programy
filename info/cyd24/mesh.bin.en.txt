A messenger for your own MeshCore node, over Bluetooth LE - the board is the HANDSET and the node is the radio. Private conversations and channels, typing on an on-screen keyboard, notification by buzzer and LED, conversation history on the card in /mesh/. It works without a node too: it shows the last conversations from the card and says outright why there is no connection. IT DOES NOT OVERWRITE OR CONFIGURE THE NODE - that is not a declaration in a comment but a whitelist of nine read-only commands in the code, guarded by a test. The companion protocol byte layouts come from the MeshCore firmware source, not the documentation - in three places the docs say something different from the code. Theme, language and calibration come from K-OS. Host tests: 249 checks with ASAN and UBSAN, including a pass over all 256 frame codes. NOT TESTED ON HARDWARE. Still missing: node configuration, maps, channel management and a landscape version. 0.1.1: THE PROGRAM STOPPED STALLING. Connecting to the node BLOCKS the main loop, and in 0.1.0 there was no pause between attempts at all - with an unresponsive node the program stood still most of the time. Now an attempt lasts at most 4 s instead of 10, and the pause between failed attempts grows from 1 s to 30 s, returning to the shortest after a success. The BLE scan went down from 60 to 19 percent duty - the node advertises several times a second anyway, and the interface regained its smoothness. The node screen now shows THE NUMBER OF DISCONNECTS AND THE REASON FOR THE LAST ONE, because the status bar only says 'node out of range', and that looks the same when the node is far away, when it refuses pairing and when it already has another client. 0.2.0: PIN AND NODE CHOICE IN THE SETTINGS. A node with a PIN other than 123456 simply ended the connection itself - the screen said only 'node out of range', while the disconnect code was 531, 'the other side ended it'. The PIN is now typed on the keyboard (an empty field = do not answer a pairing request). The program also stopped being tied to one node: the hard-coded address is gone from the code, and the settings hold a list of recognised nodes in range - you can leave 'any' or point at your own, so the board does not stick to someone else's standing closer. Changing the PIN or the node breaks the current connection at once and tries again. ...
